# Evidence and measurement for decision reviews

Draft supporting reference. Read when designing metrics, comparing tools/services, interpreting a study, or making a consequential claim that needs benchmarking. Do not load it for every routine decision.

## Separate provenance

Label consequential statements as verified fact, internal observation, external benchmark, inference, proposed target or unresolved question. Never infer zero from missing data. Record the source, date, applicable population/method and exact limitation. Primary vendor documentation can establish what a vendor says; it does not independently establish efficacy, superiority or upstream permissions.

Use responsible-platform documentation for technical capabilities, policies and restrictions; original research and recognized standards where relevant. Customer interviews and actual usage establish different things from market commentary. Never imply access to analyst reports or private competitor results that were not read. Recheck changeable information when a decision depends on it.

## Define metrics before using them

For a useful metric specify the decision it informs, definition, unit or numerator/denominator, eligible cohort, time window, version, exclusions, evidence and unknowns. Distinguish active time from elapsed time, human effort from machine activity, contribution from gross/net profit, required approval from avoidable intervention, and first sale from renewal.

A target needs a provenance label. Derive operating budgets from customer needs and actual economics, or explicitly call them hypotheses. Do not retrofit a source to make an invented number look authoritative. Critical acceptance policies such as no unsupported material final-report claims are not claims of perfect model accuracy or infallible review.

## Compare fairly

Match use case, customer segment, service level, accounting definition and observation period. Use the same raw inputs and quality requirement when comparing workflows. Count failures, corrections, review cost and setup separately. Record when a comparison is based on vendor claims or samples rather than observed operation.

Small qualitative usability rounds diagnose problems; they do not establish population completion rates. An initial handful of AI comparisons can refine a rubric, not prove superiority. For consequential quantitative claims, choose representative samples and required precision before evaluating; disclose uncertainty and avoid repeatedly checking results until a preferred answer appears.

For AI comparisons, use a credible simpler baseline, blinded/randomized order, concrete rubric examples and representative held-out cases. Capture ties and reviewer disagreement, test missing/conflicting evidence, and calibrate automated judges against qualified human judgments where appropriate. Record version, cost and latency. A separate agent is another AI evaluation, not a human/customer test.

## Challenge scores and conclusions

For consequential rankings, vary plausible weights and assumptions; explain whether the recommendation changes. Note correlated criteria and avoid false precision. A prerequisite can have a low strategic score and still need to happen first. An observed visibility change is not proof of causal improvement, revenue or customer value.

State the strongest credible counterargument and what evidence would reverse the conclusion. When evidence is inadequate, recommend a bounded test or a reversible next step rather than manufacture certainty or continue broad research without purpose.
