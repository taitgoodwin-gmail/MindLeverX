# Buyer psychology and I-O psychology — behavioral-science lens

Finalized as decision guidance on 17 September 2026. This proposes applications and tests; it does not implement a design, run a customer experiment or establish a psychological effect in MindLeverX.

## Seventh tenet

Use consumer psychology for buyer attraction, evaluation, purchase, engagement and renewal; use I-O psychology for human work, teams and adoption. Combine these under evidence-informed behavioral science to help customers and operators understand choices, take useful action and adopt effective ways of working. Consider the behavior, context, barriers and outcome explicitly; support informed choice and durable customer value.

Use this lens for decisions involving customer action, onboarding, communication, report comprehension, adoption, workflow, feedback or change. A routine technical edit does not need a psychology explanation. UX asks whether the experience is clear and usable; this lens asks what makes a person able, willing and practically equipped to act. The overlap is useful but should not count twice as independent evidence in scoring.

## Relevant disciplines and limits

- **I-O psychology:** people and work, role clarity, workload, feedback, training, coordination and organizational adoption. SIOP describes the field in terms of workplace performance and well-being. Apply this to the owner's work and human customer teams; an AI agent is not a human employee or evidence of a validated team intervention.
- **Consumer psychology:** the primary buyer-facing branch. Study what makes an offer relevant, credible and worth choosing; how buyers interpret evidence, prices and expectations; and how the delivered experience affects continued use and renewal. The Society for Consumer Psychology includes judgment, persuasive communication, information processing and consumer-brand relationships in its research scope. This establishes disciplinary fit, not effectiveness of a particular sales tactic.
- **Behavioral economics and decision science:** supporting lenses for choices, uncertainty, value, timing and friction. Specific effects need context-matched evidence and testing; naming a bias is not proof. “Marketing psychology” is a useful applied description, not a reason to bypass research quality.
- **Human factors and UX:** understandable information, interaction demands, errors and recovery. Evaluate observed usability alongside behavioral outcomes.

These distinctions select relevant methods; they do not make the assistant a credentialed psychologist or establish an effect for a particular customer. Avoid diagnosing or assigning personality types from sparse behavior. Start with explicit preferences, tasks and observed friction.

## Buyer-facing priorities for this skill

Use consumer psychology as the lead for acquisition and retention, with the following candidate questions. These are proposed applications, not verified behavioral effects.

| Buyer stage | Question to investigate | Candidate design/test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attract | Does the right buyer recognize their own problem in our message? | Compare clear outcome/problem framing; assess qualified interest and comprehension rather than clicks alone. |
| Establish trust | What uncertainty prevents consideration? | Show an actual sample, scope, evidence and limitations; observe which questions remain. |
| Evaluate and buy | Can they compare the offer with their alternatives and understand the commitment? | Test clear package comparisons, deliverables, pricing and next steps; track comprehension with purchase completion. |
| Activate | How quickly do they experience something useful after paying? | Make the first report lead to an understandable finding and feasible next action; observe time to perceived value. |
| Stay engaged | What interaction helps their business, and when is it useful? | Surface meaningful changes and decisions through chosen channels; measure useful action and support burden, not required logins or screen time. |
| Renew | Has the continuing service earned another purchase? | Make delivered value and next-cycle purpose clear; examine actual renewals, cancellation reasons and unexpected-charge complaints. |

For B2B MindLeverX, identify the actual buyer, budget approver and report user rather than assuming they are the same person. Consumer decision-making applies to the individual; organizational adoption and handoffs may also require the I-O lens. Validate roles with the customer instead of inventing personas.

## A lightweight behavior brief

For a meaningful behavioral proposal, record:

1. **Actor and action:** who should do what, when and in which context? Define a useful customer/owner outcome as well as the business outcome.
2. **Barrier hypothesis:** use observations/interviews to distinguish understanding or skill, practical opportunity/access, and motivation/value. COM-B provides capability/opportunity/motivation as a diagnostic framework; its original application does not prove effectiveness in our commercial setting.
3. **Candidate change:** choose a small change linked to the suspected barrier. Explain the proposed mechanism, evidence and transfer limitations. EAST can prompt ideas involving ease, salience/attractiveness, truthful relevant social information and timing; it is not a guarantee or a checklist requiring all four.
4. **Measurement and countermeasure:** define the action denominator and window, quality/comprehension and customer effort, plus complaints, regret, opt-outs or review burden as relevant. A click or purchase increase alone cannot establish a beneficial experience.
5. **Validation and reversibility:** choose observation/prototype review for early diagnosis; a controlled test for causal claims when feasible and authorized. Keep the change reversible and define what would cause us to revise or stop it. Low traffic is not solved by claiming certainty from a tiny A/B test.

Preserve transparent terms, genuine choice and straightforward cancellation. Use real proof and accurate progress; avoid fabricated scarcity/testimonials, concealed recurring charges or friction that traps customers. Those are design-quality choices consistent with the owner's low-friction requirement, not a new approval process.

## MindLeverX ideas to explore, not approved features

| Moment | Desired behavior and barrier hypothesis | Candidate intervention | Observation / countermeasure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | Appropriate prospect understands relevance; GEO jargon may obscure value. | Lead with the customer question, explain what the audit measures and show a real sample with limits. | Can the person explain the offer and its limits? Qualified interest alongside mistaken expectations. |
| Offer and checkout | Customer makes an informed purchase; scope, timing or ongoing charges may be unclear. | Plain-language deliverables, transparent one-time/recurring choice and expectations before payment. | Comprehension, qualified completion, abandonment reasons, refunds and later surprise. |
| Onboarding | Customer provides needed context once; a long technical form may block completion. | Essential initial inputs, save/resume and justified optional details at the relevant moment. | Active minutes, missing/incorrect information, assistance and re-entry; guard against shifting work to the operator. |
| First report | Customer chooses a feasible next action; findings may feel overwhelming or unactionable. | One clearly explained priority with evidence, effort and an optional action plan. | Reader can explain and choose/defer an action; record completion obstacles, not just report opens. |
| Client team implementation | A chosen action gets done; unclear ownership or authority may block it. | Optional named owner, agreed next step and realistic timing, with client control. | Accepted actions completed / accepted actions due; workload and useful outcomes. Commitment/reminders require actual authorization. |
| Monthly service | Customer sees continuing value; repeated dashboards may not explain what matters. | Explain meaningful change, stable conditions, uncertainty and the next useful decision. | Usefulness, actual renewal, complaints and cancellation effort; avoid requiring unnecessary logins as engagement. |
| Owner approval | Owner makes a sound review decision without overload. | Concise exceptions, evidence behind each material finding and clear approve/return actions. | Review minutes and substantive errors caught; approval speed cannot replace quality. |
| Human team coordination | People understand responsibility and can raise problems early. | Clear responsibilities, workload-aware handoffs and constructive feedback. | Handoff rework, delays, workload and reported blockers. Do not apply human motivation theories to agents as factual psychology. |

## Proposed assessment criteria

Behavioral design is well supported when the target action is explicit, the barrier has evidence, the mechanism fits the context, the choice remains understandable and voluntary, and an observed improvement serves the user as well as the business. Until tested, label it a hypothesis. No default conversion lift, personality score or new weighting is specified.

Potential metric definitions:
- **BEH-01 barrier evidence:** target actor/action/context and barrier recorded, distinguishing observed from assumed causes.
- **BEH-02 informed action:** eligible people completing the action with demonstrated understanding / all eligible people offered the action, with missing understanding and outcome observations shown separately.
- **BEH-03 follow-through:** accepted actions completed / accepted actions due by the observation cutoff; include effort and reasons for deferral.
- **BEH-04 counter-effects:** complaints, reversals/refunds, opt-outs, misunderstanding and owner/client burden alongside the desired action. Set criteria from baseline and the actual intervention.
