# MVP roadmap assessment against all seven tenets

Finalized 17 September 2026. Canonical principles and current qualitative rubric: [Product and business tenets](product-tenets.md). All ratings describe intended contribution, not measured performance or completion. Historical six-criterion arithmetic is retained at the end; it no longer ranks current priorities.

## Outcome and interpretation

The proposed MVP is a trustworthy paid audit followed by a repeatable recurring service: website URL → essential intake and authorization → payment → evidence collection → report preparation → review/approval → private delivery → recurring monitoring. Exact package, price, customer segment and one-time audit release policy remain decisions. Monthly brief owner approval is already required. Monitoring plus prioritized recommendations remains a proposed initial recurring offer; customer-site implementation was not selected.

Design and validate the owner and client journeys alongside the first real report. Do not defer UI/UX to a cosmetic pass after backend development. Recurring value and workload must be considered from the start, even though repeat delivery is the final validation stage.

These are roadmap milestones, not newly created or authorized development tasks. Existing MIN-5/6/7 gates govern the current unattended queue. Design the recurring experience early; execute only when dependencies and authorization are satisfied. Required approval is useful control: measure its clarity and avoidable effort rather than automatically penalizing UX for having it.

| 1. Validate evidence and measurement | **Behavior:** distinguish buyer questions and context from assumptions; test essential intake comprehension. **AI:** detect evidence gaps and justify questions. **Revenue:** establish a baseline for future comparisons. **Repeatability:** standard questions, evidence fields and scope. **Automation:** structured exports and quality checks. **Leadership:** traceable findings and explicit limits. **UX:** hide unnecessary provider setup and technical details from customers; make owner exceptions actionable. | Owner should not reconcile exports or provider accounts every cycle. Preserve necessary consent and security. | End-to-end test of authorization, confirmed payment, job status, review and recipient-scoped delivery; failed/duplicate jobs and payment events handled safely; test customer can complete core journey without coaching. | 5. Deliver and evaluate one paid pilot | **Behavior:** observe understanding, acceptance and barriers; collect regret and workload alongside conversion. **AI:** analyze feedback and diagnose recurring errors. **Revenue:** observe actual payment and interest in ongoing value. **Repeatability:** identify exceptions. **Automation:** measure remaining manual work. **Leadership:** obtain feedback on meaningful differentiation. **UX:** observe where a real customer or owner gets stuck. | Record assistance rather than silently masking it. ## Verified starting point and remaining uncertainty

- Marketing site was previously deployed; current paid service readiness is not established.
- Local report builder was tested with fictional input; a completed real-evidence report has not been accepted.
- On 17 September, signed-in Otterly trial access was verified: four days remaining, 15/50 prompts configured. One existing prompt export retained 16 nonempty answers, four each labeled chatgpt, perplexity, google and copilot, dated 14–17 September. This is an export qualification sample, not a representative completed audit. See the [local evidence sample](../artifacts/unattended-work/evidence/2026-09-17-otterly/qualification-sample.md) and [manifest](../artifacts/unattended-work/evidence/2026-09-17-otterly/manifest.json).
- Provider method/retention qualification, internal panel specification, real-report review, production payment/private delivery, actual paid pilot and renewal are still outstanding. No assumption of leadership, acceptable usability, margin or fully unattended delivery is validated by the planning scores.

## Recommended next sequence

1. Finish narrow evidence qualification using the existing Otterly data. Map the smallest owner/client journey and define report comprehension checks alongside this work.
2. Produce the first supported real report, then evaluate both report usefulness and owner review effort. Reuse the existing builder where it fits.
3. Use those findings to finalize the initial offer and recurring boundaries before implementing the paid workflow. Set a delivery target using measured effort and actual pilot availability.

Evidence collection and report work retain their existing authorization boundaries. This document creates no execution tickets, purchases, deployment, website changes, automatic publishing or new standing automation.
