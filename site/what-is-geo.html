What is Generative Engine Optimization?

The playbook for getting your brand cited, quoted and recommended by the AI answer engines your buyers now ask first — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude.

Reading time · 7 MINUpdated · JUL 2026Methodology · V1.0
ON THIS PAGEDefinitionGEO vs SEOWhy nowSignalsFAQTakeaways
The definition, in one paragraph

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of engineering a brand's content, technical setup and off-site presence so that generative answer engines — like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude — cite, quote and recommend it inside their answers. Where SEO competes for a ranked link, GEO competes to be the answer itself.

01 · The shift

Search changed. Optimization had to follow.

FIG. 02 · THE UNIT OF VISIBILITY
2004–2023Ranked link
↓
NOWCited answer

For twenty years, winning search meant ranking a link that a human then clicked. Generative engines broke that contract: they read the sources, synthesize an answer, and hand the buyer a conclusion — often citing only a handful of pages, sometimes none at all.

That shifts the goal. The unit of visibility is no longer a blue-link position; it's whether the model names you and quotes you when it composes the answer. GEO is the discipline of earning that — layered on top of the SEO fundamentals that still make your content credible and crawlable.

It is not a trick or a prompt. It's the same authoritative, well-structured content that ranks — engineered further so a machine can lift a clean, self-contained, attributable answer straight from your page.

02 · Comparison

GEO vs. SEO — same foundations, different finish line.

GEO doesn't replace SEO; it extends it. The technical hygiene and quality signals overlap, but what you're optimizing for — and how you measure it — changes.

05 · FAQ

Frequently asked.

Q1Is GEO just SEO with a new name?
No. GEO shares SEO's technical foundations — crawlability, structure, quality — but optimizes for a different outcome: being cited inside a generated answer rather than ranking a link a human clicks. The overlap is real, which is why GEO layers on top of SEO instead of replacing it.
Q2Which engines does GEO target?
The generative answer engines your buyers use — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's Gemini / AI Overviews, and Claude — plus the AI features now embedded in classic search. Because each synthesizes answers differently, GEO monitors visibility across all of them rather than optimizing for one.
Q3How do you measure GEO if there are no rankings?
With answer-engine monitoring: citation rate (how often your pages are cited), mention rate (how often your brand is named), and share of voice (your citation share versus the category). The sample readiness scorecard illustrates a possible reporting format. Daily engine collection and a validated scoring rubric are not connected in this build.
Q4How long until GEO shows results?
There is no delivery or outcome guarantee in this preview. A meaningful comparison requires actual collection dates, a fixed prompt panel and enough evidence to separate a change from ordinary engine variation.
Q5What does a GEO engagement actually involve?
The intended workflow agrees scope, preserves a baseline, reviews findings, records approval and prepares changes before measuring again. The seven-stage method separates those steps and identifies what is not connected yet.
06 · Reference
Key takeaways
  • GEO examines visibility in AI answers. The aim is to understand mentions and citations; inclusion is not guaranteed.
  • Keep the SEO foundations. Google says its existing SEO practices apply to AI features in Search. Eligibility does not guarantee selection, and other engines need separate evidence.
  • Review off-site evidence. Third-party references are one area to inspect; this is not a claim that they have the largest effect.
  • Preserve evidence for citations, mentions and share of voice. Compare readings only when the panel, methodology and provenance match. Missing dimensions remain unmeasured.
