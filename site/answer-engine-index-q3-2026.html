The deliverable

Inside an evidence-led audit.

A useful report connects each finding to its evidence and explains what to do next. This outline shows the structure, without fictional scores or engine results.

01 · Scope

The question the audit answers.

Record the business question, prompt-panel version, agreed surfaces, collection conditions and exclusions. The scope and fixed quote are agreed before payment.

02 · Evidence

What was actually observed.

Preserve the answer, source references, collection date and conditions. Keep brand mentions separate from citations. Distinguish model-API results from observations of consumer interfaces.

Evidence record fields
  • Buyer question and panel version
  • Surface, interface and observation date
  • Recorded response and source references
  • Observed mention or citation, with the counting rule
  • Failed attempts, missing evidence and limitations
03 · Interpretation

What the evidence supports.

Separate measured findings from inferred explanations and unmeasured dimensions. Report counts and denominators when the evidence supports them. Compare readings only when the method and conditions make comparison meaningful.

04 · Recommendations

A practical next step.

Link each recommendation to a finding, explain its rationale and state what a follow-up observation would test. A recommendation is not a guarantee of citation, ranking or revenue.

05 · Review and delivery

One approved report revision.

The owner reviews the exact report revision before private delivery. Changed evidence creates a new revision for review. Implementation and repeat monitoring are separate decisions.
